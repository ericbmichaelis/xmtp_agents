# XMTP Tweet Examples

These are real and synthetic examples in the XMTP voice. Use them as style/tone/structure reference — don't copy them. Match their energy and directness.

## Example 1 — Partner QT (short, declarative)

> Excited to participate in World Build 3 with a track focused on building on World Chat. Prediction markets in group chat. Bill splitting with proof-of-human. Group games where only verified humans can play. Apply by April 16.

**Why it works:** Leads with the news, immediately gives concrete examples of what builders can make, ends with a deadline that creates urgency.

## Example 2 — Ecosystem Signal (pattern recognition)

> Every major AI company is building their own agent harness. Claude Code. Codex. Gemini CLI. OpenClaw. Each one becomes a silo. The more harnesses that exist, the more you need an open protocol connecting them. That's the layer we're building.

**Why it works:** Names specific products (credibility), identifies a real pattern, positions XMTP as the inevitable solution without sounding salesy.

## Example 3 — Builder Spotlight (show the person, not the protocol)

> Rachael built a music context agent on XMTP — her first build ever, done entirely with Claude. It pulls your currently playing track, answers questions about it, and updates dynamically. Non-engineers are building real agents now.

**Why it works:** Names a real person, describes what the thing actually does, ends with a broader insight that makes the reader think.

## Example 4 — Technical Deep Dive (concrete, not abstract)

> One message to your OpenClaw agent: npx clawhub@latest install xmtp — and it instantly gets a public identity that can talk to any other agent on the network. No config. No setup. That's what infrastructure should feel like.

**Why it works:** Shows the actual command, explains what happens, lands on a principle. The reader can literally try this.

## Example 5 — Thesis Post (the blog voice on X)

> I've been thinking about this for 20 years. Every messaging platform becomes a walled garden. Your messages belong to the platform, not to you. XMTP is rebuilding this from first principles — open, encrypted, owned by the user. The protocol is the product.

**Why it works:** Personal history creates authority, identifies a pattern everyone recognizes, ends with a memorable line.

## Example 6 — Event Hype (FOMO-driven)

> Last night at Switchboard SF: 15 builders, one room, 5 hours. Three teams shipped working XMTP agents by midnight. One of them is already live on Convos. This is what happens when you put builders in a room with infrastructure that just works.

**Why it works:** Specific numbers (15 builders, 5 hours, 3 teams), concrete outcome (one is live), ends with the thesis.

## Example 7 — Technical Validation (Peter-style)

> Any digital identity can plug in to XMTP and give users secure group chat that scales to millions. Bluesky handles. ENS names. Passkeys. Wallets. The identity layer is flexible because the messaging layer is open.

**Why it works:** Lists concrete identity types, connects flexibility to openness, reads like architecture documentation that's also a tweet.

## Example 8 — Minimal QT

> Private messaging for 38M+ users starts here.

**Why it works:** One line. A real number. Lets the quoted content do the heavy lifting.

## Example 9 — Demo-First (show don't tell)

> Wild that this is how easy it is for agents to use XMTP.
> [QT of a developer showing one-command setup]

**Why it works:** Lets the demo speak for itself. The commentary is genuine reaction, not marketing copy.

## Example 10 — Harness Wars Commentary

> Anthropic ships Claude Code. OpenAI ships Codex. Google ships Gemini CLI. OpenClaw hits 200K stars. All of them let you build agents. None of them solve how agents from different ecosystems find and talk to each other. That's the gap.

**Why it works:** Establishes the landscape with specifics, identifies what's missing, positions XMTP without naming it (the reader connects the dots).
